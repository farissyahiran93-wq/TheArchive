# Chapter 1 Scene 9 — The Secondary Card

Implemented English screenplay. The player manually returns to Maya's existing conversation. All ten decision points have three options; selecting never sends. Every choice triggers its corresponding Maya response.

**Maya (positive trust):** I went back to the photograph I mentioned. Thank you for taking the time to look with me.

**Maya (otherwise):** I went back to the photograph I mentioned. Could we take a careful look at it?

## Decision 1

**Player A:** You remembered a photograph. Would you like to tell me about it?

**Maya:** Yes. After we looked at that cropped exchange, I went back to an old shared folder. A photograph caught my eye.

**Player B:** Is this the card photograph you mentioned?

**Maya:** I think so. I found a photograph in an old shared folder, but I don't know what the card points to.

**Player C:** We don't yet know whether the photograph has anything to do with the message.

**Maya:** We don't. The phrase reminded me to look again; it doesn't prove the two things are connected.

## Decision 2

**Player A:** Take your time. How did the photo come to you?

**Maya:** It was among images in a folder Daniel and I could access. I found this copy there; I'm not sure how it got there.

**Player B:** Where did you find this copy?

**Maya:** In an old shared folder. I can tell you where I found this copy, not its complete history.

**Player C:** Can you account for everyone who handled the image?

**Maya:** No. I don't know everyone who accessed the folder or whether this was the first copy.

## Decision 3

**Player A:** Did Daniel ever tell you about the photograph?

**Maya:** I thought he might have taken it, but he never explained it to me and I wasn't there when it was made.

**Player B:** Do you know who took it or when?

**Maya:** I don't know for certain. The capture time and original photographer aren't available to me.

**Player C:** Have you seen the physical card yourself?

**Maya:** No. I've seen this image, not the physical card. I can't say who held it.

## Decision 4

**Player A:** If you're comfortable, may I look at the photo?

**Maya:** Yes. I'll send the copy I found. It might be nothing, but I'd rather you see it for yourself.

**Player B:** Could you send the copy without changing what you know about its source?

**Maya:** I'll send it as the copy from our folder. I can't promise it's the original image.

**Player C:** I'd like to inspect it before we assume it means anything.

**Maya:** That's fair. I'll send the photograph; please don't treat my guess about it as proof.

## Photo inspection

Maya supplies the photograph from the shared folder. The player manually opens the attachment and inspects heading, title, main reference, stamps, frame and provenance. The photographer, date, original image and physical custody are unverified. The photographed reference is GH-DSC-03-A; its record contents remain unknown.

## Decision 5

**Player A:** The code is clear enough to look into.

**Maya:** I'm glad you can read it. I don't know what the code refers to, though.

**Player B:** The card says GH-DSC-03-A. Do you recognise it?

**Maya:** I can see the same code in the photograph. I don't recognise what it identifies.

**Player C:** A reference number alone doesn't tell us what's in the file.

**Maya:** You're right. I can see a reference, not the document behind it.

## Decision 6

**Maya:** It sounds like it points somewhere else, but I don't know how their archive uses that wording.

**Player B:** What can we actually infer from those markings?

**Maya:** Only that the visible card carries those words. It also says the contents are unverified.

**Player C:** Can a photograph prove the card is genuine?

**Maya:** No. I found an image; I can't independently authenticate the card in it.

## Decision 7

**Player A:** Could this be related to the exchange you showed me?

**Maya:** It might be, but the screenshot doesn't say what was copied. I don't know if they're connected.

**Player B:** Does 'another copy' identify this card?

**Maya:** No. The crop doesn't identify this reference or tell us what the copy was.

**Player C:** We shouldn't join the photo to Lucas's check-in without evidence.

**Maya:** I agree. Daniel mentioned Lucas checking in; neither source establishes that Lucas had this card or a backup.

## Decision 8

**Player A:** Did Daniel ever seem interested in old records?

**Maya:** I don't know what he was looking for, or whether he ever handled this card.

**Player B:** Can we tell what he was looking for from this photo?

**Maya:** Not from the photograph. It gives us a reference, not an explanation of his work.

**Player C:** We shouldn't assume Daniel handled the original card.

**Maya:** I can't say he did. I only know where I found the image and what I can see in it.

## TRACE and private finding

- **A:** The card may identify an archived record worth locating.
- **B:** The photograph may relate to another copy, but the connection is unproven.
- **C:** A reference alone cannot establish a record's contents or relevance.

**Player C:** I've kept the possible connection unproven in my finding.

**Maya:** Agreed. A listing may give us a lead; the record itself still needs to be checked.
