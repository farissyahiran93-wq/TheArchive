# Chapter 1, Scene 1 — A Message from Sophie

**English screenplay draft · 5 November 2026.** Each player choice requires selection and Send. A/B/C express different tones; no reply assumes a personal relationship with Daniel. Branch-specific responses converge at the next numbered beat. Sophie knows only what she found in the shared folder and what the player sends. This draft is not a playable implementation.

## Incoming message

**Sophie:** Hi. Sorry to message you out of nowhere.

**Sophie:** My name is Sophie Mercer.

**Sophie:** Can I ask if you know someone called Daniel Mercer?

### 01 — A stranger

- **A — gentle:** “I don't think so. Is everything okay?”
  - **Sophie:** “Honestly, I'm not sure. But thank you for answering.”
- **B — direct:** “No. I don't know anyone by that name.”
  - **Sophie:** “Okay. I didn't want to assume you did.”
- **C — cautious:** “Who is Daniel, and why are you asking me?”
  - **Sophie:** “That's fair. I should explain before asking anything else.”

**Sophie:** He's my brother. My older brother.

**Sophie:** I'm trying to reach people who might know where he is.

### 02 — Daniel

- **A — concerned:** “Has something happened to him?”
  - **Sophie:** “I don't know. That's the part I can't stop thinking about.”
- **B — practical:** “What do you mean, where he is?”
  - **Sophie:** “He's gone quiet, and no one close to him has been able to reach him.”
- **C — reserved:** “I'm sorry, but I really don't know him.”
  - **Sophie:** “I understand. I'm not asking you to pretend otherwise.”

**Sophie:** He hasn't answered my messages or picked up when I've called.

**Sophie:** That's not enough to tell me what's happened. I know that.

**Sophie:** But the people who know him are worried too.

### 03 — The number

- **A — curious:** “How did you get my number?”
  - **Sophie:** “From a contact export in a family folder Daniel shared with me.”
- **B — wary:** “Why is a stranger asking me about her brother?”
  - **Sophie:** “Because I found your number in a contact export in Daniel's shared family folder.”
- **C — sympathetic:** “I want to understand. Where did you find my number?”
  - **Sophie:** “In a contact export inside a family folder Daniel shared with me.”

**Sophie:** It didn't have a name beside it. Just the number and a little star.

**Sophie:** I thought it might belong to someone he knew.

**Sophie:** I can't tell you who put it there. I shouldn't have made it sound like I could.

### 04 — No connection

- **A — clear:** “I've never met Daniel.”
  - **Sophie:** “All right. I'll take your word for it.”
- **B — thoughtful:** “I don't recognise his name or have any connection to him.”
  - **Sophie:** “Then the number being there doesn't make much sense. But I believe you.”
- **C — guarded:** “A number in a file doesn't mean I know him.”
  - **Sophie:** “You're right. It doesn't. I'm sorry I put that on you.”

**Sophie:** I've been looking at that folder for so long that every little thing starts to feel important.

**Sophie:** That doesn't make it your responsibility.

### 05 — Boundaries

- **A — kind:** “I can listen, but I can't promise I'll be able to help.”
  - **Sophie:** “That's more than fair. I won't ask you for promises.”
- **B — private:** “Please don't share my number or anything I say without asking.”
  - **Sophie:** “I won't share either without asking you first.”
- **C — firm:** “I need you to respect that I might not want to be involved.”
  - **Sophie:** “I will. You can tell me to stop, and I'll listen.”

**Sophie:** I know you're a stranger, too. You have every reason to be careful.

### 06 — What she can say

- **A — concerned:** “How are you holding up?”
  - **Sophie:** “Not very well, if I'm honest. I keep checking my phone even when I know it hasn't made a sound.”
- **B — factual:** “What do you actually know so far?”
  - **Sophie:** “That I can't reach him, and the people close to him can't either. Beyond that, I don't want to guess.”
- **C — careful:** “Are you sure you want to tell me more?”
  - **Sophie:** “Only what I can say without putting words in anyone else's mouth.”

**Sophie:** Daniel usually tells me when he's going to be unavailable.

**Sophie:** He doesn't always remember to call when he says he will. But he comes back with an apology and some terrible excuse.

**Sophie:** I would give anything for one of those excuses right now.

### 07 — A small group

- **A — warm:** “He sounds like someone you care about a lot.”
  - **Sophie:** “I do. Even when he's being impossible.”
- **B — measured:** “I can see why the silence worries you.”
  - **Sophie:** “Thank you. I'm trying to keep worry from turning into assumptions.”
- **C — skeptical:** “Silence doesn't tell us where he is.”
  - **Sophie:** “No. It doesn't. That's why we're trying to put together what we actually know.”

**Sophie:** A few of us have a group chat called FIND DANIEL.

**Sophie:** It's for sharing what we've heard and keeping track of questions. Nothing more official than that.

### 08 — The invitation

- **A — curious:** “Who's in the group?”
  - **Sophie:** “Me, Maya and Lucas. Maya is Daniel's fiancée. Lucas is his best friend.”
- **B — cautious:** “Would everyone there see what I write?”
  - **Sophie:** “Yes. It's a group chat with me, Maya and Lucas. Please don't post anything you wouldn't want all of us to read.”
- **C — open:** “What would you need from me?”
  - **Sophie:** “Mostly, an honest answer if anything looks familiar. And it's okay if nothing does.”
  - **Sophie:** “The others are Maya and Lucas. Maya is Daniel's fiancée; Lucas is his best friend.”

**Sophie:** I can invite you, if you want.

**Sophie:** You don't have to join just because I asked.

### 09 — Decide without pressure

- **A — willing:** “All right. I'd like to see the invitation.”
  - **Sophie:** “Okay. I'll send it. You'll still get to decide whether to join.”
- **B — uncertain:** “I need a little time before I decide.”
  - **Sophie:** “Of course. I'll leave the invitation here so you can look at it whenever you're ready.”
- **C — decline for now:** “I'd rather not join right now.”
  - **Sophie:** “I understand. I won't add you. If you change your mind, the invitation will still be here.”

**Sophie:** And thank you for replying, even if this is where we leave it.

## Invitation card

**FIND DANIEL** — Sophie, Maya and Lucas.

Actions: **JOIN GROUP** or **NOT NOW**. JOIN GROUP is the explicit joining action and saves Scene 1 completion once. NOT NOW dismisses the card; **View invitation** restores it without joining. A declined or postponed decision never adds the player. On joining, unlock Scene 2 and leave navigation manual; no Scene 2 messages are delivered by this screenplay.

## Authoring and delivery notes

Every A/B/C option is an actual player message, saved only on Send, and receives its matching Sophie response. Suggested relationship effects: A empathy +1 affinity where emotionally appropriate; B neutral; C privacy/caution neutral. No choice blocks progression or converts a reasonable boundary into hostility. All lines and effects need stable new-route IDs and actual receipts. Suggested pacing uses short separate bubbles with existing typing and reduced-motion behavior. The invitation is one persistent object, not a repeated message on reload. The player must manually open Sophie's message. Deliver no Unknown, TRACE, recovered archive, invented sighting, future revelation or Scene 2 dialogue.
