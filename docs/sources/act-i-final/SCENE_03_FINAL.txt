## DIRECTOR'S NOTES
- Slow-burn does not mean padding. Leave room for a memory, a joke, a silence and a choice to land; no machine-like flood of all three at once.
- Sophie reveals **Daniel is her older brother** because intimacy has been earned, not as an exposition dump. His ruined birthday cake and “I'll explain later” habit make the absence personal.
- Maya Chen is **Daniel's fiancée**, Lucas Grey is Daniel's childhood friend, Eli Ward is a **woman** and technical colleague at Axiom Recovery. This scene introduces their human roles but does not pre-judge them.
- Optional sender disclosure is available ONLY if player actually opened the private message request. If not, Sophie never mentions it. If player shares it, she knows what the player quotes, NOT the sender's identity.
- Keep **Nora Vance**, the article's byline and contents, the older copy and recovered records for Scene 04–07. Scene 03 has only Sophie's mention of a public article; the link is deliberately withheld until the next scene.
- Sophie can be wrong about events, but she is not written as lying by default. Lucas does not become a villain simply by leaving the group; Maya's deleted draft does not become an incriminating file.
- No romantic foreshadowing with Sophie is implied by a warm conversation. Affinity changes measure rapport, not consent or romance.
- If Sophie does not know player name from Scene 01, use no [PLAYER_NAME] substitutions in her dialogue. Future conditional address only if knowledge flag true.
