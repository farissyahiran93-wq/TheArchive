# 3. DEFINITIVE SECRET STORY — WRITER KNOWLEDGE ONLY

This section contains the actual truth behind the mystery.

Do not expose these facts to players prematurely.

## June Ellis

June discovered discrepancies between original structural safety reports and published approval records relating to the Greyhaven Harbor Renewal project.

She began collecting evidence and requested Daniel's assistance.

Daniel trusted Rowan Bell and disclosed information about a planned meeting with June.

Rowan passed this information to Vivian Hale.

A contracted security operative attempted to retrieve June's original evidence. During the confrontation, June fell and sustained fatal injuries.

Her death was subsequently concealed as an accident.

Vivian knew that the true circumstances had been covered up.

## Daniel Mercer

Daniel blamed himself for exposing June's meeting.

He continued investigating June's death, collecting messages, records, logs, and original document references.

He asked his coworker Eli Ward to preserve an encrypted backup of relevant phone data.

Daniel later attempted to obtain a physical copy of the original records before bringing the evidence to Detective Rachel Hayes.

He was intercepted and kidnapped by security operatives connected to Vivian's cover-up.

Daniel is held alive at an old harbor storage facility because the perpetrators still need information about the hidden original evidence and recovery keys.

Daniel claims that evidence may be released if he fails to complete a check-in, making the kidnappers reluctant to kill him.

The entire playable investigation should occur over approximately 7–10 days, with a documented timeline that makes the captivity and recovery plausible.

## Eli Ward / Unknown Sender

Eli is afraid of being exposed because he previously failed to report suspicious system access.

He possesses Daniel's encrypted backup and contacts the player anonymously.

He does not know Daniel's exact holding location.

His motivations include guilt, concern for Daniel, fear of retaliation, and the desire to preserve evidence.

He introduces TRACE as a means to inspect and recover parts of the archive.

## The Starred Number

The player has no prior personal relationship with Daniel.

The player's phone number appears in a manipulated copy of Daniel's contact records.

The manipulation is part of an attempt to misdirect, monitor, or compromise the investigation.

A separate warning card states:

"DO NOT CONTACT THE STARRED NUMBER."

At the end of Chapter 1, Sophie reports discovering the card and says:

"But… I already did."

The exact origin of the player's number, how it was obtained, who physically produced the warning card, and how the card reached Sophie must be logically resolved in the Story Bible before implementation.

Do not rely on coincidence or magical foreknowledge.

## Ending

Daniel is rescued alive in Chapter 5 by the authorities under Rachel Hayes.

The cover-up surrounding June's death and manipulated archival records is exposed.

Rowan must face the consequences of his actions and may cooperate with investigators.

Vivian's role is established through corroborated evidence.

Daniel eventually messages the player to express his gratitude.

The final ending should provide emotional closure for Sophie, Maya, Daniel, and the player.

# 4. TRACE PHONE RECOVERY SYSTEM

Daniel's encrypted phone archive is recovered progressively.

The cumulative recovery targets are:

- Chapter 1: 20%
- Chapter 2: 40%
- Chapter 3: 60%
- Chapter 4: 80%
- Chapter 5: 100%

The percentages represent recoverable content within a defined archive manifest, not the percentage of the mystery solved or all data ever present on Daniel's physical phone.

The recovered data categories include:

- Messages
- Notes
- Activity logs
- Gallery
- Call history
- Voice recordings
- Deleted fragments
- File metadata
- Selected archived records

Recovery is progressive and persistent.

For example, a message first encountered in Chapter 1 may contain unrecovered sections. Additional fragments recovered in Chapter 2 or later should be integrated into that same message record.

TRACE must never fabricate missing message content.

Use clear labels such as:
- RECOVERED
- PARTIALLY RECOVERED
- UNRECOVERED
- CORRUPTED
- SOURCE UNVERIFIED
- INFERENCE ONLY

TRACE functions must evolve across chapters:

Chapter 1:
- Initial file recovery
- Read messages and notes
- Evidence summary
- Basic timeline

Chapter 2:
- Additional fragment recovery
- File comparison
- Photo metadata
- Compare backup sources

Chapter 3:
- Timeline reconstruction
- Source correlation
- Contradiction analysis

Chapter 4:
- File provenance
- Version history
- Access records
- Tamper analysis

Chapter 5:
- Final reconstruction
- Complete archive review
- Evidence synthesis
- Final case report

Evidence originating from Sophie, Maya, Lucas, Nora, Rachel, public records, or other external sources must be distinguished from the Daniel phone archive. External findings must not artificially increase the phone recovery percentage.

# 6. PLAYER-DRIVEN GAMEPLAY REQUIREMENTS

TheArchive must not be a passive visual novel in which events automatically complete without player interaction.

Required interaction categories:

1. Receive and open chats.
2. Choose dialogue responses.
3. Accept or decline group invitations.
4. Manually open private messages.
5. Inspect evidence attachments.
6. Review and install TRACE with consent.
7. Manually start recovery operations.
8. Read recovered content.
9. Use TRACE summaries with direct source references.
10. Build timelines from evidence.
11. Compare conflicting sources.
12. Save findings.
13. Review before sending sensitive information.
14. Send acknowledgments or reports when required.
15. Answer incoming calls.
16. Return Home when required by scene progression.
17. Review evidence boards and previously recovered files.

Optional actions must remain optional.

Required story gates must be clearly distinguishable from optional interactions.

Do not silently auto-send messages, auto-join groups, auto-install software, or auto-open evidence.

Choices should modify relationships, dialogue, evidence presentation, and reactions without permanently locking the player out of the main mystery.

Provide credible alternative routes where necessary.

# 7. NARRATIVE QUALITY RULES

Every scene must have:

- Clear story objective.
- Start trigger.
- Active character(s).
- Player interaction.
- New information or dramatic development.
- Optional dialogue branches where useful.
- Evidence inspected or acquired, if applicable.
- Meaningful scene completion conditions.
- Ending beat or cliffhanger.
- Connection to the following scene.

Do not use filler conversations solely to increase scene count.

Each scene should create at least one of:
- New credible evidence.
- New relationship development.
- New contradiction.
- New risk or obstacle.
- Meaningful change in what the player believes.

Daniel must feel like a real person with relationships, mistakes, fears, routines, and regrets.

Sophie should behave like a concerned younger sister.

Maya should behave like Daniel's fiancée.

Lucas's privacy conflict must remain consistent.

Eli should not behave like an omniscient AI. He knows only what he has personally observed or learned.

June's death should be handled seriously, with realistic investigative and emotional consequences.

No character should know facts without a credible source.

Preserve uncertainty whenever evidence is incomplete.

The Greyhaven Bulletin should remain useful through Ana Pierce and the June Ellis investigation, but do not repeat the former three-scene Bulletin version-history investigation.
